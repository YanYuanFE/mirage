# Refraction

**A visual philosophy of the seen-but-unfixed.**

Refraction is the discipline of the almost-there. It studies the moment a solid
thing meets a gradient of heat and bends — present to the eye, impossible to
pin. The work lives in that bending: a form whole enough to name, dissolved
enough to slip the grasp. Nothing hides; everything shifts. This is the paradox
the philosophy renders with master-level precision — legibility and evasion
holding the same body at the same instant.

Form is built from strata. A single disc is cut into horizontal bands and each
band is sheared along a slow wave, so the silhouette breathes rather than
sits. The eye completes the circle the bands refuse to close. This is the
signature move, executed with painstaking attention: the outline is never
drawn, only implied by the disciplined drift of parallel lines. Every offset is
calculated, never sketched — the product of deep expertise in how the eye
forgives a broken edge and still sees a whole.

Color carries temperature, and temperature carries meaning. A cool jade holds
the top — the shielded, the private, the held-back — and warms toward amber at
the base, the exposed and the public. The gradient is a horizon: cool sky over
hot sand, the exact atmosphere in which a mirage is born. The two hues are never
mixed muddily; they are calibrated to pass through one clean teal midpoint, a
transition meticulously tuned so the descent from cool to warm reads as
inevitable rather than decorative.

Rhythm is everything and ornament is nothing. The bands repeat with the patience
of ruled paper, and the wave that shears them is the only gesture — one sine,
carried with restraint across the whole height. Negative space between the
strata is not empty; it is the haze itself, the shimmer made of what is missing.
Spacing is measured to the sub-pixel, the kind of quiet exactitude that only
reveals its labor on the second, closer look.

Text, when it appears, is a whisper set beside the form: a serif wordmark of
optical elegance, a mono caption of clinical calm, a reference marker that
pretends the mark is a specimen under study. Words anchor; they never explain.
The composition must communicate through proportion, interval, and the exact
angle of the shear — the way a master's work proves itself without a caption.

The result should read as an artifact labored over for countless hours: crisp to
the edge, cohesive to the token, every band and every stop the outcome of
relentless refinement. Not decorated — resolved. A mark that behaves like the
thing it names: seen everywhere, found nowhere.
